    Notes on method

    Most plates are integrated with the classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta method at fixed steps of a few milliseconds of simulated time or less. The cyclotron uses the Boris scheme, which rotates velocity exactly in a magnetic field. The orbit uses leapfrog with steps that shrink near periapsis, and the cart is balanced by a linear–quadratic regulator whose gains are solved from the Riccati equation whenever you change a mass.

    Every line on a plate has a meaning: springs are zigzags, dampers are pistons in cylinders, hatching marks a fixed support. Where a value can be computed live it is shown in the strip beneath the figure, substituted into its own equation.

    Plates pause when they leave the screen. Use the speed menu to slow anything down, and the parameters drawer to break it.
